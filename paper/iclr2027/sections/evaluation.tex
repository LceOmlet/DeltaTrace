\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\paragraph{Tasks and fixed responses.}
We evaluate Qwen3-8B on all 1,243 examples in the authors' \texttt{table1-data-v1} release accompanying FlashTrace \citep{pan2026flashtrace}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/wbopan/flashtrace/releases/tag/table1-data-v1}} The selection contains ten RULER tasks with 100 examples each, HotpotQA with 48, MATH with 100, and MoreHopQA with 95. Deletion faithfulness scores the complete released response plus EOS, which is also the attribution target for \dt{}. Its input formatting, eligible positions, gold mapping, and FP16 eager scoring follow the released code. The frozen \texttt{clean-v1} method uses the shared rules in Section~\ref{sec:method}; the faithfulness comparison includes published baseline aggregates. VT and HotpotQA recovery use the separate re-evaluation specified below.

\paragraph{Source recovery and deletion faithfulness.}
NIAH recovery uses the top 10\% of released eligible tokens. VT reports body-token Recall at budgets of 10\%, 10\%, 20\%, and 30\% for H2, H4, H6, and H10. HotpotQA reports supporting-fact Recall with a 10\% body-token budget. Faithfulness uses the authors' 20-step deletion procedure: RISE ranks signed contributions and MAS ranks their positive part; lower is better. Appendix~\ref{app:evaluation} specifies the recovery protocols.

\paragraph{Complete task results.}
Across seven methods, \dt{} achieves the lowest RISE on 9 of 13 tasks and the lowest MAS on 11 (Tables~\ref{tab:full} and~\ref{tab:mas}). Against FlashTrace, the corresponding improvements cover 10 and 12 tasks. The largest absolute RISE reduction occurs on MATH, from 0.3484 to 0.2029. Recovery is highest on all six NIAH tasks, with gains over FlashTrace of 3.96--19.60 percentage points. At the stated VT budgets, \dt{} reaches an 84.18\% macro Recall versus 76.57\% for FlashTrace K3. On HotpotQA, IFR has the highest mean, 75.87\%, versus 72.05\% for \dt{} (Table~\ref{tab:recovery}). These comparisons describe observed means.

\paragraph{Qwen3.5-9B results.}
Table~\ref{tab:qwen35-quality} compares symmetric GDN propagation with FlashTrace on 60 NIAH cases with new key/value assignments over fixed templates, plus six fixed released examples each from MATH and MoreHopQA. \dt{} has lower RISE on six of eight tasks and lower MAS on seven. Recall is higher on all six NIAH tasks. FlashTrace has lower RISE on MQ-Q4 and MQ-Q8, and lower MAS on MQ-Q8. On MATH and MoreHopQA, both RISE and MAS are lower with \dt{}. These are descriptive results on the stated subsets; Appendix~\ref{app:evaluation} specifies their construction.

\paragraph{Measured attribution cost.}
Figure~\ref{fig:efficiency} reports complete attribution calls on Qwen3-8B, including input preparation, endpoint capture, layer replay, propagation, and returning scores. On the 16-example benchmark, the sum of per-example median latencies is 11.40 seconds for \dt{} and 12.16 seconds for one-hop FlashTrace; maximum allocated memory is 18.76 and 21.49 GB, respectively. Appendix~\ref{app:cost} specifies the measurement protocol and longer-input comparisons.

\paragraph{Signed evidence in concrete responses.}
Figures~\ref{fig:retrieval} and~\ref{fig:multihop} show the first released example from each development task with the same fixed response evaluated by both models. The retrieval view connects two source keys to their numbers and to the query naming those keys. The multi-hop view follows the context linking a voice actor to a film and its director, whose surname determines the answer. Teal and coral retain the direction of each token's contribution to the complete response score. Aligned context and question excerpts show the evidence, and full-input strips locate it in the surrounding context. The accompanying deletion curves compare each model's positive-ranking DT attribution with its own one-hop FlashTrace baseline, measuring the normalized log-likelihood of the entire fixed response plus EOS.
