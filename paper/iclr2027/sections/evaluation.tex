\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
We evaluate evidence recovery, deletion faithfulness, and attribution cost.

\paragraph{Tasks and methods.}
We first evaluate Qwen3-8B on the 1,243 examples released with FlashTrace \citep{pan2026flashtrace}. These comprise ten RULER tasks with 100 examples each, HotpotQA with 48, MATH with 100, and MoreHopQA with 95.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/wbopan/flashtrace/releases/tag/table1-data-v1}} Needle-in-a-haystack (NIAH) tasks retrieve information among distractors. Multi-query tasks (MQ-Q2/Q4/Q8) ask for 2, 4, or 8 keys, while multi-value tasks (MV-V2/V4/V8) ask for 2, 4, or 8 values of one key. Variable Tracking (VT) follows variable assignments. Here H is the number of binding hops and C the number of parallel chains. HotpotQA and MoreHopQA require combining facts, while MATH contains mathematical reasoning problems. The complete stored reasoning and answer plus EOS is scored for faithfulness. We compare \dt{} (DT) with FlashTrace (FT), Perturbation, REAGENT, Context Length Probing (CLP), Information Flow Routes (IFR), and Attention-aware Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (AttnLRP). DT and FT use the same evaluation examples. VT/HotpotQA recovery compares all seven methods.

\paragraph{Evidence recovery.}
Recall is the fraction of annotated evidence recovered among the highest-ranked sources. NIAH evidence consists of the inserted key--value sentences, and VT evidence consists of the assignment statements along the target variable chain \citep{pan2026flashtrace}. A token budget limits how much input the ranking may select. For NIAH, DT attributes the complete response plus EOS. We select the top 10\% of eligible input tokens ranked by positive-part attribution scores. Eligible tokens are the source positions marked for evaluation in the released examples. VT measures token Recall in the passage body, with budgets of 10\%, 10\%, 20\%, and 30\% for H2, H4, H6, and H10, and attributes the answer tokens. HotpotQA reports Full-support Recall@30\%. An example scores one if every official supporting fact is selected, and zero otherwise. It attributes the full response, ranks passage sentences by the sum of their positive token contributions, and selects the longest ranking prefix within 30\% of all body tokens. FT uses three recursive tracing steps (K3) for recovery. Table~\ref{tab:recovery} reports these values.

\paragraph{Deletion faithfulness.}
The released deletion test replaces high-ranked source tokens with EOS in 20 steps, rescoring the same complete response after each step. The score is normalized between the full-input and fully-deleted endpoints. A good ranking causes an early score drop because it removes evidence the model uses. The RISE deletion score summarizes the area under this curve. Magnitude Aligned Scoring (MAS) also compares the remaining response score with the remaining share of positive attribution \citep{pan2026flashtrace}. Lower values are better for both metrics. DT uses signed scores for RISE. For MAS, it sets negative scores to zero, ranking sources by their positive support for the response. Appendix~\ref{app:evaluation} specifies the ranking and normalization.

\paragraph{Attribution quality.}
On Qwen3-8B, DT has the lowest RISE on 10 of 13 tasks and the lowest MAS on 11 (Tables~\ref{tab:full} and~\ref{tab:mas}). The largest absolute RISE reduction against FT is on MATH, from 0.3487 to 0.2321. DT achieves the highest Recall on all six NIAH tasks and all four VT tasks. At the stated VT budgets, its macro Recall is 87.75\%, compared with 76.57\% for FT K3. On HotpotQA, DT reaches 85.42\% Full-support Recall, compared with 75.00\% for FT K3 (Table~\ref{tab:recovery}).

\paragraph{Qwen3.5-9B results.}
We also evaluate DT and FT on the hybrid Qwen3.5-9B model using 13 tasks comprising 520 examples, including all 400 VT and 48 HotpotQA examples (Table~\ref{tab:qwen35-quality}). DT has lower RISE on 10 tasks and lower MAS on 12. Recall is higher on all six NIAH and all four VT tasks. On HotpotQA, DT reaches 95.83\% Full-support Recall, compared with 58.33\% for FT K3, and lowers both RISE and MAS.

\paragraph{Attribution time and memory.}
The first-page timing panel varies response length on Qwen3-8B. DT, FT, and multi-hop FT (FT-mh) are measured on one MetaX C550. The other curves show the published timing references. Crosses mark out-of-memory runs. In the 16-example Qwen3-8B benchmark, the sum of per-example median attribution times is 11.40 seconds for DT and 12.16 seconds for one-hop FT. Peak allocated memory is 18.76 and 21.49 GB. On Qwen3.5-9B, processing the 16 examples in batches of two with GPU checkpoints reduces time from 16.68 seconds for serial execution to 12.33 seconds, a 35.3\% throughput increase. Peak memory is 21.64 and 23.88 GB, respectively. Times cover complete attribution calls. Appendix~\ref{app:cost} gives the measurement protocol.
