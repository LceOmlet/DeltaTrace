\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\paragraph{Tasks and fixed responses.}
We evaluate Qwen3-8B on all 1,243 examples in the authors' \texttt{table1-data-v1} release accompanying FlashTrace \citep{pan2026flashtrace}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/wbopan/flashtrace/releases/tag/table1-data-v1}} The selection contains ten RULER tasks with 100 examples each, HotpotQA with 48, MATH with 100, and MoreHopQA with 95. Deletion faithfulness scores the complete released response plus EOS, which is also the attribution target for \dt{}. The original input formatting, eligible source positions, gold mapping, and FP16 eager response scoring follow the released code. The frozen \texttt{clean-v1} method uses the shared rules in Section~\ref{sec:method}; the comparison includes published Perturbation, REAGENT, CLP, IFR, AttnLRP, and FlashTrace results, preserving their reported attribution construction and aggregation.

\paragraph{Source recovery and deletion faithfulness.}
Recovery@10\% measures the fraction of gold evidence within the top 10\% of eligible input tokens. Faithfulness uses the authors' 20-step deletion procedure and released RISE and MAS functions. RISE ranks the complete signed contributions; MAS and source recovery use their positive part. Lower RISE and MAS indicate better deletion faithfulness. We report recovery on the six multi-query and multi-value retrieval tasks. Appendix~\ref{app:evaluation} specifies the metric views and reference-result provenance.

\paragraph{Complete task results.}
Across seven methods, \dt{} achieves the lowest RISE on 9 of 13 tasks and the lowest MAS on 11 (Tables~\ref{tab:full} and~\ref{tab:mas}). Against FlashTrace, the corresponding improvements cover 10 and 12 tasks. The largest absolute RISE reduction over FlashTrace occurs on MATH, from 0.3484 to 0.2029; MoreHopQA improves from 0.1280 to 0.0697. Recovery is highest on all six retrieval tasks (Table~\ref{tab:recovery}), with gains over FlashTrace of 3.96--19.60 percentage points. The more demanding MQ-Q8 task illustrates that recovery and deletion capture distinct aspects of attribution: recovery rises from 7.50\% to 13.24\%, while RISE and MAS increase. These comparisons describe observed task means. The DT evaluation retains every released example; baseline means follow the published task results.

\paragraph{Hybrid-model development results.}
A separate paired development set contains the first eight MQ-Q2 and eight MoreHopQA examples for each model. On Qwen3.5-9B, recovery rises from 63.92\% to 73.35\%; MAS decreases on both tasks. RISE is 0.1115 versus 0.0942 on MQ-Q2 and 0.1688 versus 0.2356 on MoreHopQA. These results exercise the same finite rules through the hybrid model's attention and gated-memory paths. Table~\ref{tab:development} gives the complete development comparison for both models.

\paragraph{Measured attribution cost.}
Complete attribution calls include input preparation, endpoint capture, layer replay, propagation, and returning scores. Loading and warmup are recorded separately. On the 16-example Qwen3-8B implementation benchmark, the sum of per-example median latencies is 11.40 seconds for \dt{} and 12.16 seconds for one-hop FlashTrace; maximum allocated memory is 18.76 and 21.49 GB, respectively. On Qwen3.5-9B, real batches of two examples with GPU checkpoints reduce the mean time per 16 examples from 16.68 to 12.33 seconds, increasing throughput by 35.3\%. The corresponding allocated-memory peaks are 21.64 and 23.88 GB. Appendix~\ref{app:cost} records the benchmark snapshots, repetition protocol, longer-input tiling measurements, and additional replay-retention results.
