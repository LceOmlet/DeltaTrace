\begin{abstract}
We introduce \dt{}, which assigns signed scores to input words to explain a fixed, complete reasoning response. It compares the response's log-probability under the original prompt and a reference prompt. Analytic rules convert the observed activation changes into coefficients for one reverse pass, analogous to gradient backpropagation. The token scores sum to the response-score difference. The rules follow content and control together. Attention traces values and their selection, while gated delta memory traces stored content and the keys, queries, and gates controlling retention, writing, and reading. Analytic normalization accounts for competition among attention sources. FlashAttention-style tiling and Flash Linear Attention chunks give sequence-linear auxiliary storage at fixed model dimensions and chunk size. On Qwen3-8B retrieval and reasoning tasks, \dt{} achieves the lowest deletion-faithfulness scores (RISE and MAS) on most tasks and the highest needle-in-a-haystack Recall, exceeding FlashTrace by 5.67--24.33 percentage points. On Qwen3.5-9B, across 13 tasks and 520 examples, \dt{} lowers RISE on 10 tasks and MAS on 12 relative to FlashTrace.
\end{abstract}
