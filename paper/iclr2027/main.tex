\begin{abstract}
We study how words in the context support or oppose a language model's reasoning and final answer. Holding this response fixed, \dt{} measures how its log-probability changes when the context tokens being explained are replaced by a common reference token. It assigns this measured difference to those tokens by modifying backpropagation. For each operation, \dt{} uses its formula and values from both executions to express the observed output change as a weighted sum of input changes. These weights replace local derivatives in the backward pass, distributing the contribution assigned to each output among its inputs. The input contributions sum to the output contribution at every step, so the final token scores sum to the measured response-score difference. Positive and negative scores indicate support and opposition. This gives one explanation spanning the reasoning and answer, which can also be aggregated into sentence or passage contributions. The method handles language models with attention or linear attention. Its auxiliary memory grows linearly with sequence length at fixed model dimensions and chunk size. On the evaluated attention model, \dt{} achieves the lowest deletion-faithfulness scores (RISE and MAS) on most retrieval and reasoning tasks and the highest needle-in-a-haystack Recall, exceeding FlashTrace by 5.67--24.33 percentage points. On the evaluated hybrid attention and linear-attention model, across 13 tasks and 520 examples, \dt{} lowers RISE on 10 tasks and MAS on 12 relative to FlashTrace.
\end{abstract}
