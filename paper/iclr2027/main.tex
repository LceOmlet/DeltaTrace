\begin{abstract}
We study how words in the context support or oppose a language model's reasoning and final answer. Holding this response fixed, \dt{} measures how its log-probability changes when the context tokens being explained are masked. \dt{} modifies backpropagation because slopes at a single point generally cannot recover this finite change. It derives local rules from each operation's formula and the two executions, then composes them in one reverse pass into effective secant slopes from input embeddings to the response score. Multiplying embedding displacements by these slopes and summing within each token gives its signed contribution. Local conservation makes these contributions sum to the measured score difference and preserves their meaning when aggregated over sentences or passages. The method handles language models with attention or linear attention. Its auxiliary memory grows linearly with sequence length at fixed model dimensions and chunk size. On the evaluated attention model, \dt{} achieves the lowest deletion-faithfulness scores (RISE and MAS) on most retrieval and reasoning tasks and the highest needle-in-a-haystack Recall, exceeding FlashTrace by 5.67--24.33 percentage points. On the evaluated hybrid attention and linear-attention model, across 13 tasks and 520 examples, \dt{} lowers RISE on 10 tasks and MAS on 12 relative to FlashTrace.
\end{abstract}
