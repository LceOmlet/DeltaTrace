\begin{abstract}
We introduce \dt{}, which assigns signed scores to input words to explain a complete reasoning response. It compares the response's log-probability under the original prompt and a reference that replaces source words with end-of-sequence tokens. Analytic rules convert the observed activation changes into coefficients for one reverse pass, analogous to gradient backpropagation. The token scores sum to the response-score difference. The rules follow content and control together: attention traces values and their selection; gated delta memory traces stored content and the keys, queries, and gates controlling retention, writing, and reading. Analytic normalization accounts for competition among attention sources. FlashAttention-style tiling and Flash Linear Attention chunks give sequence-linear auxiliary storage at fixed model dimensions and chunk size. On Qwen3-8B retrieval and reasoning tasks, \dt{} achieves the lowest deletion-faithfulness scores (RISE and MAS) on most tasks and the highest needle-in-a-haystack Recall, exceeding FlashTrace by 3.96--19.60 percentage points. Qwen3.5-9B development measurements evaluate the hybrid-model implementation; batching with GPU checkpoints increases throughput by 35.3\%. The same reference-based formulation motivates policy learning, where exactly averaged action-level return differences recover the advantage signal.
\end{abstract}
